# Portable fork patch map

Baseline: `1c59fa65e43d706fef2f0d8de21cc76b7cd27a22` (merged Beignet 0.27.0 release). `package.json` records `upstreamVersion` and `upstreamCommit`, and the bundle reports that version through `GET /api/config`. Published-tarball qualification is pending.

The 0.26.0 to 0.27.0 resync uses a per-file three-way merge against pristine `026d202b`. The workspace-root backup is `pre-send-max-20261004.tgz`. Across the source tree, 270 paths were unchanged, 23 took upstream changes, 15 retained an unchanged fork patch, and seven merged changes from both trees. The only adjacent conflict was in `BeignetNodeOptions`; the final declaration retains both the injected `irohFactory` and upstream `waiveClientReserve` setting. All 22 previously patched paths remain present, including the fork-only reconstructable batch module. Timer compatibility, SQLite batching, injected transport, Iroh fallback, direct-funding null handling and diagnostics are retained. The merged release has the same `src/` tree as the reviewed integration commit `dedac49c8a8538d8320b84b54ecf078db3d96437`.

Upstream adds destination-aware splice quotes, exact send ceilings, frozen-debit pay-all, negotiated reserve waivers, external cooperative close history and durable fixed-input sweeps. These accounting and protocol implementations are taken from upstream. The embedded configuration sets the wallet role, advertises the waiver feature and always keeps `waiveClientReserve` false.

Portable additions expose the new routes and implement a durable drain coordinator outside `src/`: exact reviewed coins and fees, an owner-bound channelize hold, mutation fencing, restart and reorg reconciliation, and a single public Activity with both transaction IDs. Its public revision counters let clients reject stale progress. The private journal retains the engine's exact sweep identity and is never returned through Activity or configuration.

`portable/net.ts` now buffers inbound chunks when a consumer is between data listeners or paused, with a 2 MiB bound and ordered delivery after listener registration. This prevents the synchronous relay from discarding the tail of a 65,535-byte peer-storage frame during the real peer's init handoff. Adapter regressions cover listener gaps, pause/end ordering and overflow; a real Peer encrypted-framing regression covers the large frame followed by a later message.

Validation before publication: 167 portable tests pass, public types and build pass, and dependency audit reports zero findings. The new `regtest-send-max.cjs` runs sequential disposable cases for JIT max, frozen later receipts, restart, drain, three address scripts, opener cost and CLN reserve contrast. Live qualification against the published tarball remains required. See README for its environment variables.

What 0.23.1 changes for this fork. Receiving (#1067): `createJitInvoice` sizes the intent it registers with the LSP by the share that must cross the LSP, the amount less what the other channels the invoice advertises can receive (a channel's balance above the reserve, counted only for a channel the invoice would hint and never for a channel with the LSP itself), and registers no total once those channels could carry the whole amount; before, every fixed-amount JIT intent was registered with the whole amount, and a payer that delivered part of it over the wallet's other channel left the LSP waiting for a total that never arrived and failing every held part after its 60 s window. This is the case of a phone that keeps a usable channel to a peer other than its primary, the old primary after a switch being the payer in the upstream report; the portable router calls the daemon-level `createJitInvoice`, so it takes the new sizing with nothing to forward, and upstream's own note names this fork as needing it for Chicory. Balances (#1066): `pendingCloseBalanceSats` leaves a FORCE_CLOSED channel's figure in the same read in which the wallet history first holds the sweep of its balance output, so the sats the wallet already counts on chain are no longer counted a second time for the hundred blocks the channel stays FORCE_CLOSED (the phone showed 10,924 sats against 5,297 held), and a peer's own to_local on its commitment no longer pins the channel FORCE_CLOSED. Channels and broadcasts (#1069): each `ChannelInfo` carries `pendingSpliceTxid` (present exactly when `pendingSpliceLocalBalanceSats` is) and `previousFundingTxids` (the fundings retired by adopted splices, oldest first), both in display order; `channelsWithFunding` spreads the object it annotates, so both reach `GET /channels` unchanged, which coreyphillips/beignet-wallet-core#16 waits on. `BROADCAST_FAILED` and `BROADCAST_PERMANENT_FAILURE` name their transaction (`txid`), its channel and whether the node still holds and re-sends it on every block (`retained`), a permanent failure being the watcher's retry queue giving up rather than the end of a retained attempt, and a splice rebroadcast the backend refuses raises `SPLICE_BROADCAST_REFUSED` once per (txid, reason) with the reason; all three reach the runtime's `node:error` listener as hex `channelId`, `txid` and `retained` through `wireNodeErrorRelay`. What the runtime does with them for the splice-out journal is fork issue #7, recorded below. Closes (#1068): a channel close or sweep pays the wallet's change chain, never the next receive address, which is the address `POST /address/new` handed out last and the one a saved receive request may hold, so a payout can no longer be read as that request's payment; the offline leg reads `Wallet.getChangeAddress()`, whose on-the-fly path used to answer `ok(undefined)` on a wallet that had not yet set its indexes and now returns the generated address. Payments (#1071): a `fee_insufficient`, `incorrect_cltv_expiry` or `amount_below_minimum` failure that carries a verified `channel_update` re-prices the erring channel for that payment's retries (`policyOverrides`, scoped to the payment and never written to the graph) instead of excluding it, the first-hop diversification on a retry keeps the only local channel that can carry the amount instead of excluding it, and `route.hops[].feeMsat` on a payment record reports the fee each hop kept. A splice-in during a running splice is refused up front as `SPLICE_BUSY` (#766) instead of accepted and failed later with wallet inputs pledged: `portable/channelize.cjs` already turns a refused splice-in into a failed decision and waits `CHANNELIZE_RETRY_MS`, so a second deposit confirming while one splice is still confirming now waits for the next pass instead of leaving a submission that fails asynchronously. Overlapping keyed sends broadcast once (#768) and CORS preflights allow `X-Idempotency-Key` (#769); the portable router is in-process, has no CORS and no idempotency cache, so neither applies to it. A direct-funding payer can ask the receiver to replay a lost receipt with the opt-in `recoverReceipt` (#767); the portable `POST /direct-funding/send` forwards only the request and amount and does not expose it yet, so a portable wallet that lost a receipt keeps its recorded outcome as before. The fix for beignet's own splice-in refusal wording and the interop and test-helper changes (#603, #666, #364) are upstream-only.

The node's onion send hook swallowed every `PeerManager.sendToPeer` throw, so a payer whose peer view listed the introduction node but whose link refused the write (a peer no longer ready for messaging, an outbound gate) counted an offer that never left the process; the transport registry, which correctly refuses to switch lanes once a frame was exchanged, then never fell through to the synthesized blind relay, and the payer sat out the 120s offer timeout reporting `EXCHANGE_TIMEOUT` for a local refusal. Nothing in the portable runtime relied on the swallow: `portable/runtime.ts` never sends onion messages itself.

`portable/runtime.ts` (2026-09-13, none of it touches upstream `src/`): a primary that did not answer at start is dialled again on a short backoff (5, 15 and 30 seconds) before the minute poll takes over, and `setup()` no longer runs twice at once. The engine's `node:error`, `peer:error`, `peer:connect` and `peer:disconnect` events reach the client's diagnostic hook from before the first dial rather than after it, so the first connection is reported like every later one, and the hook now reads the peer events' two arguments (pubkey, error): the error's own message and code are what the client sees, where before it saw only the pubkey, and the two-second redial after a primary error keys on the pubkey argument it was actually given. The React Native app records these events with timestamps and appends them to its Diagnostics card; that log is what showed a phone's first connection after a cold start going dead for forty seconds before a redial made the channel usable.

**Folded into the 0.21.0 baseline (was ahead of 0.20.0, 2026-09-13):** `src/lightning/transport/peer-manager.ts` carries upstream's in-flight dial join (beignet #805, merged the same day as `880b1e7`): a second outbound dial to the same peer and address joins the dial in flight instead of opening a second socket. Without it, every cold start of the phone ran two overlapping dials to the primary (the node's start-up reconnect and the runtime's `setup()` connect), the primary kept the newest inbound and reset the other, the phone kept the first to complete, and the channel sat unusable until the first ping failed thirty seconds later; the primary's log showed `read ECONNRESET` for the phone at every cold start. The hunk was fork-neutral and is now part of the pristine baseline, so the fork patch no longer contains it.

`portable/runtime.ts` and `portable/offline-receive.ts` (2026-09-26, fork issues #3 and #4, none of it touches upstream `src/`): `GET /api/config` advertises `signet` in `supportedNetworks`, the one list creation also checks (`portable/network.ts` has carried the signet genesis all along, and the receive-request store already compares a signet invoice against the `tbs` prefix upstream's decoder yields); `portable-tests/runtime.test.cjs` holds the two equal. `portable/splice-status.cjs`, `portable/proof.ts` and `portable/runtime.ts` (2026-09-26, fork issue #7, none of it touches upstream `src/`): the splice-out journal now hears what the engine says about a broadcast. `BROADCAST_FAILED`, `BROADCAST_PERMANENT_FAILURE` and `SPLICE_BROADCAST_REFUSED` (beignet #1062, in the 0.23.1 baseline) name their transaction, its channel and whether the node still holds and re-sends it on every block; `watchBroadcastErrors` matches them against the `pending` and `uncertain` splice-out rows whose channel and current splice they name (the verified txid, the channel's `pendingSpliceTxid`, or the new funding of a zero-conf channel that adopted the splice; never the funding the row was submitted against), records the error on the row (`broadcast`: txid, code, reason, retained, at) and puts the engine's reason in `statusNote` ("the Bitcoin network has refused this transaction so far (reason); your wallet keeps retrying" while retained, "no longer retrying" when the engine said it does not hold the transaction). The row's status is untouched: a permanent failure is the chain watcher's retry queue giving up, not the end of a retained attempt, so no row is marked `failed` on that error alone, and unrelated rows, direct fundings, settled rows and a repeat of the same report are left alone. The per-row reconciliation moved into the same module (`reconcileSpliceRow`, with `spliceInFlight` and `STALLED_SUBMISSION_MS`), unchanged in what it does except for two things: the candidate it verifies now includes the channel's `pendingSpliceTxid`, so a confirm-first splice is verified against the chain once it is fully signed rather than after its lock, and a server that answers that it does not have the transaction (an adopted zero-conf splice the network has never seen; `queryElectrum` now marks that answer `CHAIN_EVIDENCE_REFUSED` and `verifySubmission` returns `{ matched: false, unseen: true }` for it instead of throwing) puts "the Bitcoin network has not seen this transaction yet; your wallet keeps rebroadcasting it" on a row older than the stalled interval, once, and leaves it `pending`. The stalled timer keeps its conditions and stays the fallback for a loss the engine never reported: a row whose channel shows no splice in flight and an unchanged funding still becomes `uncertain`, and a server that could not be asked changes nothing, as before. `portable-tests/splice-status.test.cjs` drives a fake node: a retained error for a row's channel and splice txid sets the reason and keeps the row pending while an unrelated row, a direct funding and a settled row are untouched; `SPLICE_BROADCAST_REFUSED` replaces the note with the backend's reason and a second identical report does not save again; a non-retained error is still not a failure; an error about the old funding, another channel or an older payload without a txid matches nothing; an adopted zero-conf splice Electrum never returns gets its explanation after the interval and not before, once; an unreachable server leaves the row alone and a recorded broadcast reason stands over the unseen note; the stalled path still turns a row uncertain; and a verified splice promotes the row through `pendingSpliceTxid` before the lock and the funding after it.

## Upstream surface this fork does not expose

Beignet 0.15.0 added `src/lightning/swaps/` and a reverse swap provider (Lightning to on-chain); 0.16.0 added the submarine direction (on-chain to Lightning) in `src/lightning/swaps/submarine-engine.ts`. `src/lightning/node/lightning-node.ts` imports both statically, so the modules are present in the bundle, but every swap path is gated behind `config.swaps.enabled`, which the portable runtime never sets, so neither provider is ever constructed. The portable router deliberately does not expose the daemon's `GET /swaps/status`, `GET /swaps` or `POST /swaps/cancel`. Serving swaps in either direction is a provider role that locks the node's own coins in contracts for peers; both are off by default upstream and neither is a wallet feature. The engine still ships no wallet-side swap client; 0.16.0 places that in a separate client package.

The same release added a second JIT opening-fee collection mode. `POST /jit/invoice` now accepts and returns `feeMode`: `skim` deducts the fee from the delivery, `hop` bills the sender through the invoice routing hint. The shared client names `skim` on the wire and refuses any other mode, because the receive quote it showed the user priced a deduction.

0.17.0 adds an optional `cltvLimit` to the four pay routes, bounding a payment's total CLTV expiry against the current tip (a `CLTV_EXCEEDS_MAX` refusal, or `CHAIN_NOT_SYNCED` when there is no tip). It exists so a swap provider can bound a payment against its refund height; it is not a wallet need, so the portable `POST /invoice/pay-safe` does not forward it and the shared client does not send it. The release's other change, the durable zero-conf splice rebroadcast obligation, is the reason it was taken and is described in the README.

0.21.2 adds `POST /direct-funding/prepare`, which reads a request and starts dialing the node a send of it would talk to first, so a dial over Tor is not queued in front of the exchange once the user presses Send. It spends and records nothing. The portable router does not expose it: it is a payer-side latency optimisation, and the phone is the receiver in the flow this fork drives. The same release adds an optional `chainWaitMs` to the receiver policy; the portable runtime leaves it at the engine default of 20 seconds.

0.18.0 and 0.19.0 are splice-safety releases: a confirm-first splice for a stranger's direct funding with a conflict revert, and four fixes around splices that are confirmed but below their lock depth, splicing channels and HTLC backstops, retracted splice sightings, and funding spends mined below `minimumDepth`. The direct-funding receiver policy gained `allowUnpairedSplice` (default off) and `unpairedSpliceDepth`. `portable/lfbw.cjs` now sets `allowUnpairedSplice` next to `allowSplice`, matching the host manager's rules, and leaves the depth at the engine default of three confirmations, so an anonymous Beignet payer's confirmed coin grows the home channel rather than opening a second one; an anonymous payer whose coin is unconfirmed still gets a new confirmed channel, and a second such offer while one splice is confirming is declined so the payer falls back to a plain send that channelizes on its own. `portable/runtime.ts` listens to the four splice events and the accepted-offer event only to keep two fields on the wallet record for the client's notes. Enabling the live check for this exposed that the portable bundle had never minted a direct-funding envelope at all: `buffer@6` has no `base64url` encoding, the envelope encoder uses it, and the shared client turns a failed mint into a warning and a plain request. `portable/globals.ts` now maps `base64url` onto base64 for `toString`, `from`, `write`, `byteLength` and `isEncoding`, matching Node byte for byte (`portable-tests/buffer.test.cjs`). The four new splice events are not exposed by the portable router, which has no event stream; the wallet learns a splice's outcome from `GET /channels`, its own submission journal, and the two record fields above.
